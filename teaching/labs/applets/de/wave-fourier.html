Wave equation and Fourier series

A string pinned at \(x=0\) and \(x=\pi\), released from a shape you choose. No time stepping happens here. The initial data is expanded in the standing modes \(\sin(nx)\), each mode is advanced by its own \(\cos(nct)\) and \(\sin(nct)\), and the modes are summed. The sum is truncated at forty terms, so a shape with a corner in it is reproduced only to within the overshoot that truncation leaves.

\[ u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx}, \qquad u(0,t) = u(\pi,t) = 0, \qquad u(x,t) = \sum_{n\ge 1}\bigl(A_n\cos nct + B_n\sin nct\bigr)\sin nx \]
Starting from
\(u(x,0) = \sin x\)

A whole \(m\) is a mode of this string and needs one coefficient. A half-integer \(m\) is not, and the table shows how many it takes instead.

Drag across the figure to draw \(u(x,0)\). While you drag you see the drawing itself; let go and you see its forty-mode approximation. Compare the two, and note where the approximation departs from what you drew.

The string is periodic with period \(2\pi\), so the slider covers five returns to the start.

Largest coefficients

\(n\) \(\sqrt{A_n^2 + B_n^2}\)

A shape that is already a mode needs one coefficient. A shape with a corner needs every mode, and its coefficients decay like \(1/n^2\), which is slow enough to see in the table.
