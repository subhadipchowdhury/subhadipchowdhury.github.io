Wave equation and Fourier series

Let's pin a string at \(x=0\) and \(x=\pi\), pull it into a shape of your choosing, and let go. Nothing here steps forward in time. Instead we write your shape as a sum of the standing modes \(\sin(nx)\), which is its Fourier sine series, move each mode along at its own frequency with \(\cos(nct)\) and \(\sin(nct)\), and add the modes back up. The sum stops at forty terms, so a shape with a corner in it comes back slightly rounded, with small ripples to either side of where the corner was. More terms would shrink those ripples, but slowly: a corner's coefficients only decay like \(1/n^2\).

\[ u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx}, \qquad u(0,t) = u(\pi,t) = 0, \qquad u(x,t) = \sum_{n\ge 1}\bigl(A_n\cos nct + B_n\sin nct\bigr)\sin nx \]

Mode \(n\) has period \(2\pi/n\), and every one of those divides \(2\pi\), so the whole motion repeats every \(2\pi\) whatever you started from. The slider covers five returns to the start.

Largest coefficients

\(n\) \(\sqrt{A_n^2 + B_n^2}\)

A shape that's already a mode needs one coefficient and nothing else. A shape with a corner needs infinitely many, and they only decay like \(1/n^2\), which is slow enough to watch happening in the table. Try the pluck: it uses no even \(n\) at all (why?).
